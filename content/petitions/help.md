# How petitions work

The United Hampshire Parliament Petitions Service is the official route for electronic petitions to the House of Commons when the House has authorised that scheme.

**The electronic petitions scheme is not currently open.** You cannot start or sign a parliamentary petition through this website at present. A draft submitted through another website does not become a petition to the House merely because it is published there.

## What you can do now

You can [browse the public petitions record](/petitions), [view archived petitions](/archived/petitions) and [contact Parliament](https://parliament.uhrblx.com/contact/) about parliamentary business. An empty archive means that no previous Parliament petitions have been recorded here; it does not indicate that the House has decided or rejected a petition.

## Starting and signing electronic petitions

Before electronic petitions can open, the House must approve the applicable eligibility, admissibility, initial-supporter, validation, publication and other procedural rules. Those rules and any response or debate thresholds must be published and built into this service before applications are accepted.

When authorised, starting a petition will require a request for a specific action and the contact and validation information required by the House's rules. Staff check the petition against those rules before deciding whether it may be published. Submission alone will not guarantee publication, a Government response or parliamentary debate.

## Written or paper petitions

The House may provide a separate route for a formal written or Member-presented petition. It is not interchangeable with the electronic petitions process. Ask [the House Administration](https://parliament.uhrblx.com/contact/) for the applicable route and requirements.

## Help and complaints

For technical difficulties, accessibility requests, information about petitions or complaints about the administration of this service, use [Parliament's contact arrangements](https://parliament.uhrblx.com/contact/). A service complaint is not an appeal against a petition decision unless the applicable House rules provide such a review.

You can read the [current parliamentary publications](https://parliament.uhrblx.com/publications/) for information about the House and its business.
